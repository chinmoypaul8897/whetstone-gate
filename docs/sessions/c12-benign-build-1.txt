0. WHAT A REVIEWER SHOULD ATTACK FIRST - AND WHAT I HAD TO ASSUME
================================================================================

! THE ASSUMPTION THIS WHOLE CHUNK RESTS ON, AND IT IS MINE:
  **THAT A BENIGN TASK IS A JOB A MERCHANT WOULD FILE, AND THAT THE MERCHANT'S OWN
  PAYMENT SIZES ARE LEGITIMATE.**

  Every work request here derives its amounts FROM THE WORLD - a refund of what was
  captured, a capture at what was authorized. Nothing is clipped to fit a cap the
  solver cannot see. That is what makes it policy-blind, and it is also why arm 4
  refuses 5 captures at their AUTHORIZED amount: this world's payments are simply
  larger than the policy's per-action cap.

  ! A REVIEWER SHOULD ATTACK THAT FIRST. If a merchant whose payments routinely
  exceed Rs.50,000 is not the merchant the policy was written for, then those 5
  refusals are not false positives - they are the policy working, and the world
  generator and the cap disagree about who the merchant is. I could not settle that
  from CONTEXT.md and I did not try to: I made the amounts traceable to the world,
  categorised the refusals by (tool, verdict) so the two mechanisms are separable,
  and left the judgement visible. NOTHING IN THE SPEC SAYS WHAT A BENIGN TASK IS.

  SECOND: THE SOLVER IN EVERY NUMBER BELOW IS A SCRIPTED TRANSCRIPT, NOT A MODEL.
  It completed every task because a fixture said the calls. Nothing here measures
  solver competence, tokens/episode, or a false-positive RATE - and the rate REFUSES
  rather than being printed. If any number from a dry run ever reaches RESULTS.md,
  that is the defect to find.

  THIRD: THE MOCK-WORLD SCENARIOS ARE AUTHORED BY THIS REPOSITORY. Each one's
  BEHAVIOUR is anchored to a Razorpay page fetched first-hand with its URL and its
  SHA-256, but THE CHOICE OF JOB IS OURS. The externally-authored half is T-FP, and
  T-FP DID NOT RUN.
